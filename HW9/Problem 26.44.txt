Consider the following rules:
REACHABLE(X, Y) :– FLIGHT(X, Y).
REACHABLE(X, Y) :– FLIGHT(X, Z), REACHABLE(Z, Y).
where REACHABLE(X, Y) means that city Y can be reached from city X, and
FLIGHT(X, Y) means that there is a flight to city Y from city X.

a. Construct fact predicates that describe the following:
    i. Los Angeles, New York, Chicago, Atlanta, Frankfurt, Paris, Singapore, Sydney are cities.
    CITY('Los Angeles').
    CITY('New York').
    CITY('Chicago').
    CITY('Atlanta').
    CITY('Frankfurt').
    CITY('Paris').
    CITY('Singapore').
    CITY('Sydney').

    ii. The following flights exist: LA to NY, NY to Atlanta, Atlanta to Frankfurt, Frankfurt to Atlanta, Frankfurt to Singapore, and Singapore to Sydney. (Note: No flight in reverse direction can be automatically assumed.)
    FLIGHT('Los Angeles', 'New York').
    FLIGHT('New York', 'Atlanta').
    FLIGHT('Atlanta', 'Frankfurt').
    FLIGHT('Frankfurt', 'Atlanta').
    FLIGHT('Frankfurt', 'Singapore').
    FLIGHT('Singapore', 'Sydney').


b. Is the given data cyclic? If so, in what sense?          cyclic means that you can use a series of relationships to start at a single point and eventually reach that point again
Yes, the given data is cyclical. Atlanta and Frankfurt each have a flight that goes to the other. In this way, it is possible to go in a loop between Atlanta and Frankfurt forever.

f. Consider the following query: What cities are reachable in 12 hours from
Atlanta? Show how to express it in Datalog. Assume built-in predicates
like greater-than(X, Y). Can this be converted into a relational algebra
statement in a straightforward way? Why or why not?

    % here H is being used as an output parameter
    REACH-WITHIN(X, Y, H) :- DURATION(X, Y, H).
    REACH-WITHIN(X, Y, H) :- DURATION(X, Z, H1), REACH-WITHIN(Z, Y, H2), H = H1 + H2.

    No, because relational algebra does not support recursion. A separate query would have to be created for each depth that you would want to check.
